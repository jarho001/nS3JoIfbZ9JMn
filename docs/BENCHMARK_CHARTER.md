## Current player and action contract

The current reference harness runs a complete Showdown `BattleStream` battle for the format above. It generates two six-member teams with separately seeded calls to `Teams.generate`, initializes the battle with its own seed, and gives each player a policy RNG seed. The runner's current default seed configuration has five independent four-limb seeds (`teamP1`, `teamP2`, `battle`, `policyP1`, `policyP2`); each limb is an integer from 0 through 65,535. The default is a deterministic smoke sample, not a statistical benchmark suite. Current limits are 60,000 ms, 20,000 object packets, 4,000 decision attempts, and five unavailable-choice retries per request.

The policy receives its own player-scoped history and its current private request. [`environment/protocol.cjs`](../environment/protocol.cjs) projects split `update` packets by audience: each player sees its own secret branch and the public branch for the opponent. A `sideupdate` request is delivered only to its named player; spectator projection omits private side updates and uses public split branches. The projection drops `end` metadata from player views and rejects an unsupported top-level stream `error` channel. An addressed private `sideupdate` error is retained as that player's `privateEvent`; the runner classifies unavailable-choice and invalid-choice feedback and handles it as retry or failure. The policy does not receive the opponent's request, the complete packed teams, combined omniscient records, or the raw trace packet stream. Those remain trusted oracle evidence. [`reference/trace.cjs`](../reference/trace.cjs) stores trusted inputs and outputs for deterministic reference replay.

The currently supported request parsing is Gen 5 singles: a move request with one active slot and legal move slots, including the locked/recharging one-move form; a forced-switch request with one active slot and available replacements; and a non-actionable wait request. Legal choices are represented as `move N` or `switch N`. A Showdown unavailable-choice rejection invalidates that command for the same request episode, produces a retry against the updated request, and is counted separately from a newly attempted policy decision. See [`candidateActions`](../environment/random-policy.cjs) and [`runBattle`](../environment/battle-runner.cjs). This is the implemented harness contract; it does not claim a JAX environment or complete future battle API.

## Reference workload and seed procedure

**Proposed reference workload:** run complete battles through the pinned Showdown runner with the deterministic legal-random policy in both seats, preserving team generation, request projection, choice submission, retries, and terminal validation. For each base seed case, the future pair executor will call `prepareTeam` once for team A and once for team B, then pass each immutable packed team handle into two separate `BattleStream` runs: A/P1 and B/P2, then B/P1 and A/P2, with the same battle seed. Each battle stream creates its own mutable battle and Pokémon state from the shared packed team data. Keep each policy identity's policy RNG seed with that policy when swapping seats. Record both expanded five-stream tuples and the two packed-team identities, and verify each identity appears in both seats. Report the pair and each battle. Keep fixture-generation/mechanics-oracle jobs separate from full-battle throughput: they answer different questions. Never substitute one seed for another stream or reuse policy RNG state across battles.

The current default five-stream seed tuple may be used as a single smoke case and for trace reproduction. A future scored throughput suite should enumerate and publish a dedicated seed manifest. Seed tuples should be reproducibly derived from a recorded root seed with documented, domain-separated streams for team A, team B, battle RNG, policy A, and policy B; record the derivation algorithm/version and the expanded tuples. A base tuple `[teamA, teamB, battle, policyA, policyB]` pairs with `[teamB, teamA, battle, policyB, policyA]`. Paired runs hold the generated teams and battle seed fixed, as defined above.

**Unresolved:** suite size, seed roots, and statistical power/resource budgets are not set. Choose them before a scored measurement, publish the manifest, and justify the number of independent battles from the desired interval precision and available compute. Do not choose or report a target speed from the PokeJAX or Jaxcalibur figures in [`REUSE_AUDIT.md`](REUSE_AUDIT.md); their workloads and hardware are not comparable.

**Implemented manifest tooling (Task022):** [`environment/paired-seeds.cjs`](../environment/paired-seeds.cjs) emits a version 1 manifest from a required caller-supplied four-limb root seed and pair count. For stream seeds it hashes UTF-8 `JSON.stringify(["gen5-paired-seeds-v1", rootSeed, pairIndex, streamName])` with SHA-256 and reads digest bytes 0–7 as four big-endian uint16 limbs. The pair ID hashes `JSON.stringify(["gen5-paired-seeds-v1:pair-id", rootSeed, pairIndex])`; its first 16 lowercase hex digits follow a `pair-` prefix and six-digit one-based index. It emits stable pair/run IDs, team and policy identities, and both expanded runner-compatible five-stream tuples. Metadata pins the output to `reference-lock.json` and `gen5randombattle`. There is no implicit suite size; the CLI's 100,000-pair maximum is an input/allocation guard, not a benchmark budget or recommendation. The focused pinned integration test runs both configs and verifies the actual packed team written for each seed recurs in the opposite seat. Task022 verified deterministic regeneration only. Task023 adds the prepared-team API described below.

**Implemented prepared-team reuse (Task023):** [`environment/prepared-teams.cjs`](../environment/prepared-teams.cjs) prepares one immutable packed team from a four-limb seed. Its opaque handle binds the packed data to the exact Showdown object, Gen 5 format, pinned upstream commit, and seed. The handle is backed by a process-local `WeakMap` and is not serializable or restorable from its visible seed and digest metadata. `runBattle` accepts two matching handles and writes their packed data directly into the new stream, without calling the generator or packer again. It rejects forged, mismatched, cloned, or incomplete handles before opening the stream and sets `resultRecord.teamSource` to `prepared`. The handle and packed hash remain trusted engineering metadata and never enter policy input. Each use creates a new `BattleStream`, which makes independent mutable battle and Pokémon state; only immutable packed team data is shared. The per-battle `elapsedMs` starts when `runBattle` starts and excludes earlier `prepareTeam` work on this path.

## Per-battle counters and future result accounting

The runner now returns a separate schema-versioned `resultRecord` for each battle. Keep these counters distinct when a future aggregate report is implemented, and retain both completed-battle and attempted-work denominators:

| Counter | Definition for the future report | Current implementation evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Decision attempts | One actual policy invocation for one actionable player request. Count each player separately and in total; wait requests and duplicate pending requests are not decisions. | `resultRecord.counters.policyInvocations`; `summary.decisions` remains the legacy total. Decision-limit guards are checked before invocation. |
| Choice submissions | Each choice successfully sent to Showdown, including a retry submission. A policy result whose stream write fails is not a sent submission. | `resultRecord.counters.choiceSubmissions`, by player and total. |
| Accepted, rejected, pending choices | Count each sent submission in exactly one state. Acceptance is inferred from subsequent public battle progress; unresolved sent choices remain pending. | `resultRecord.counters.choiceResolutions`; accepted + rejected + pending equals sent submissions. |
| Selected and sent moves/switches | Keep policy selections separate from successfully sent choices, and report both move and switch counts by player and total. | `resultRecord.counters.actions.selected` and `.submitted`. Existing `summary.actions` keeps its policy-selection behavior. |
| Turn index | Keep the latest numbered public `|turn|` marker separate from terminal metadata `turns`. Neither is a count of policy decisions. | `resultRecord.counters.latestPublicTurn` is derived from the spectator projection; `.terminalMetadataTurns` is copied from validated end metadata and `summary.outcome.turns` is unchanged. |
| Events | Count each public `event` record once from the spectator projection. Exclude packet envelopes, `|split|` markers, timestamps/heartbeats, requests, and private side updates. | `resultRecord.counters.publicEvents`. Split secret branches never contribute. |
| Completed battles | A completed battle reaches validated public terminal output that agrees with trusted end metadata. Report wins, losses, draws, and seat separately in a future aggregate. | `resultRecord.status: "completed"` and its validated `outcome`; failed runs have `status: "failed"` and `outcome: null`. |
| Rejections, retries, exhaustion | Report unavailable-choice retry requests, successfully sent retry choices, and retry-limit exhaustion separately. A retry is not itself a policy invocation. | `retryRequests`, `retrySubmissions`, and `retryLimitExhaustions` are separate per-player counters. |
| Failures/timeouts | Keep initialization/configuration, protocol/terminal validation, policy/invalid-choice, runtime/infrastructure, packet/decision/retry limits, and wall-clock timeout separate from battle outcomes. Preserve partial counters. | `resultRecord.failure` has a stable category and code; optional `cleanupFailure` records cleanup separately. Raw error and private packet text are omitted. `elapsedMs` is present on success and failure. |

The existing smoke summary and trusted transcript remain unchanged; the timing and counters live only in `resultRecord`. Use completed validated battles per wall-clock second as an end-to-end reference rate, with decisions/s and events/s as supplementary rates, only in a future scored benchmark with reviewed suite and resource budgets. The current record's elapsed time is instrumentation and makes no throughput claim.

## Timing protocol

The current `resultRecord.elapsedMs` uses monotonic wall clock and covers the `runBattle` invocation through terminal validation and stream cleanup. On the default path this includes team generation. With prepared team handles, prior `prepareTeam` work is excluded because preparation occurs before the runner invocation; such result records set `teamSource: "prepared"` to identify that boundary. On a runner failure the timer covers work through the failure and cleanup attempt. `elapsedScope` is `battle_runner` for this boundary. If the CLI fails before `runBattle` starts, `elapsedScope` is `cli_initialization` and `elapsedMs` covers only CLI/reference initialization. It is per-battle instrumentation only.

**Proposed:** define and report separate timing boundaries for a future aggregate benchmark. End-to-end wall time starts before per-battle team generation and includes initialization, policy calls, packet handling, retries, and terminal validation. A separately labeled steady-state step measurement may exclude one-time process/setup and compilation only after an explicit warm-up phase; it must still include all work represented by the named unit. Report team-generation and startup time separately when excluded from a secondary rate.

For JAX, report compilation/first-call time separately from warmed execution. Synchronize asynchronous work before starting and after ending each timed interval (for example, block on returned arrays); say which buffers/results are synchronized. For CPU reference runs, state process reuse and concurrency. Record monotonic wall clock, device utilization where available, and peak host/device memory where available. Do not compare a JAX device rate with Showdown unless batch size, policy, transition boundary, concurrency, initialization, warm-up, and synchronization rules are all stated.

**Unresolved:** benchmark implementation, batch size, worker/process count, concurrency, warm-up count, repetitions, confidence interval method, hardware allocation, memory cap, and maximum elapsed-time budget. No timing run or measured performance claim is part of Task020.

## Reproducibility record and version changes

A future aggregate result should include:

- charter version, date, command, source workspace revision, clean/dirty status, configuration, and benchmark harness revision;
- Showdown repository and commit, resolved-format fixture hash, generator/source identity, and five expanded seed streams per battle;
- Node/JAX/Python and package versions or lockfile hashes, compiler/runtime versions, OS, CPU model, accelerator model, driver/runtime versions, and device count;
- workload definition, batch/concurrency settings, warm-up and compilation treatment, timing boundaries, synchronization rule, and resource limits;
- the per-battle records and definitions for decision/submission/resolution, selected/sent actions, public turn/events, retries, failures, and elapsed time, plus any aggregate memory counters;
- result aggregation, uncertainty interval, excluded runs and reasons, and the complete per-run data needed to recompute summaries.

Changing the Showdown revision, format rules, team generator, player projection, policy, or measurement boundary creates a new benchmark identity. Regenerate and review the format manifest and relevant oracle/trace fixtures; do not splice results across identities. The repository has reference fixtures, replayable seeded traces, and a deterministic paired-seed manifest generator. It has no statistically designed suite or stable aggregate timing record; the manifest generator takes a caller-selected root and count without selecting sample size or statistical power.

## Workload tiers and remaining gaps

Report three workloads separately; they answer different efficiency questions and their rates must not be combined:

- **Simulator-only:** batched reset/step throughput with scripted or preselected actions and no learned policy. A JAX batched full-battle simulator is not implemented. The current Showdown runner is an orchestration reference, not an accelerator simulator-only result.
- **Rollout plus policy:** complete battle collection including player projection, observation encoding, policy inference/action selection, legal-action handling, and retries. The current Showdown runner with legal-random policies is a reference workload for this tier, not a JAX implementation or measured throughput result.
- **Full training:** rollout plus trajectory storage and learner update work, including recurrent state, optimization, and evaluation hooks. This workload is not implemented for full battles.

## Current scope and remaining gaps

Implemented today: pinned Showdown reference, format metadata export, seeded team/battle/policy streams, a deterministic legal-random reference runner with limits/retry accounting, player-view projection, trusted trace replay, version 1 per-battle result records, the Task022 paired-seed manifest generator, and the Task023 seed-bound prepared-team API. `node environment/run-random.cjs --result PATH` writes result records for CLI battles; failures from `runBattle` also expose records as `error.resultRecord`. `node environment/paired-seeds.cjs --root-seed a,b,c,d --pairs N` prints explicit paired runs and their expanded seeds. A Task022 integration check verifies deterministic team regeneration in exchanged seats; Task023 prepares each packed team once and lets callers reuse those immutable strings in two independently created BattleStreams. The process-local handles are not serializable or usable across processes, and do not restore from metadata alone. Result records and manifests are trusted engineering metadata and are never policy input; policy histories remain recipient-projected. Prepared-run elapsed time excludes earlier preparation; result timing is instrumentation, not a throughput claim. Narrow JAX mechanics kernels and their fixtures are listed in [`MECHANICS_COVERAGE.md`](MECHANICS_COVERAGE.md).

Proposed here: execute a full paired manifest automatically, publish a scored seed suite, aggregate benchmark reporting, secondary timing boundaries, device synchronization, warm-up treatment, resource reporting, and version-change procedure. The prepared-team API provides reusable packed data, but no pair-execution command or aggregate benchmark exists. These protocol choices require a later implementation and review.

Unresolved for future measurements: scored sample size and statistical budget; hardware, concurrency, memory and timeout budgets; exact warm-up/repetition settings; package/runtime/device lock completeness; and long-term root/count selection and seed-manifest publication policy. These operational choices must be settled before a scored measurement where they apply, but do not automatically gate G0.

G0 specification closure remains pending review. The necessary G0 review is to confirm that the pinned `gen5randombattle` target and rules/data source basis, reuse decision, source/version manifest, and known mechanics/coverage gaps are explicit and consistent, and to resolve or explicitly bound any open decision that would change the target or its rules/data scope. The draft charter, format fixture, reuse audit, and mechanics ledger are evidence for that review; this document does not record G0 acceptance.

Later implementation gaps remain at their project gates: G1 reference-environment integration and full request/visibility/action-path differential coverage; G3 battle/request state and event/turn scheduling, coupled PRNG consumption, and representative mechanics; G4 complete reachable rules/data and full-format differential transfer. The project also lacks a full JAX battle state/transition simulator, batched complete-battle workload, trajectory collection, recurrent policy learner, and training/evaluation benchmark. The arithmetic composition is not a full battle engine. G0 does not require those later implementations, and this charter does not claim G0 or G1 completion.
